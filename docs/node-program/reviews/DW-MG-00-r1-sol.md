# MG-00 r1 independent design review

Reviewed `53a78fb1..65ced72f` in the clean `dw/mg-00-ux-spec` worktree, the 35 light PNG frames (with focused inspection of the vote, masternode, withdrawal, operator and shared-join screens), the parked governance engine, rust-dashcore's simplified list, the pinned Platform wallet, and Dash Core v24.0.0-rc.3. I assume the desktop will use the planned MG-01..MG-13 facades rather than a local full node. The manager's four settled choices stand.

## Findings

1. **Major — Current payout and payout-role data can silently become stale.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:845-850`, `docs/design/MG-00-masternodes-ux.md:146`. The rule allows a held ProRegTx or *a* ProUpRegTx to supply the displayed payout and payees, and MG-03 only calls out matching transactions that name a wallet key. A later ProUpRegTx from another wallet can change only the payout to an outside script. The simplified list has no payout field, and the new transaction need not contain the old payout or owner key; Dash Core's compact filter exposes its **ProTx hash** (`src/evo/specialtx_filter.cpp`, `TRANSACTION_PROVIDER_UPDATE_REGISTRAR`). A wallet that does not monitor that hash can show the old address as current, mislabel payout custody, and present the wrong fixed destination in `mn-withdraw`. Require MG-03 to monitor each known ProTx hash through the tip, apply every ProUpRegTx/ProUpShareTx in order (including after restore), and mark current payout/role unknown until that reconciliation completes. Dash Core applies the new payouts in `src/evo/specialtxman.cpp:1481-1501`.

2. **Major — Tracking plus a matching voting key does not establish the collateral outpoint needed to vote.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:963-970`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1094-1099` (`mn-track`, `gov-vote`). The proposed live key check uses the simplified list, but a governance vote names the masternode's collateral outpoint, which that list does not contain. The parked engine explicitly omits an otherwise controlled masternode without an outpoint (`m3/r2-governance:rust/crates/dw-engine/src/governance/voting.rs:1-9,263-267`); a newly tracked node with no prior vote and no wallet-held ProRegTx can therefore appear eligible in the mock yet be unable to cast its first vote. Specify an authenticated ProRegTx/outpoint backfill or historical filter rescan when tracking, plus a visible unresolved state until the outpoint is known. Do not precheck such a row.

3. **Major — The exact governance tally and per-node vote claims exceed the SPV data in some valid cases.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1012-1035`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1094-1099` (`gov-list`, `gov-vote`). Core tallies by collateral outpoint; the parked `dw-governance` tally maps votes by voting key because the simplified list has no outpoints. Its own comments identify two mismatches: a stale outpoint whose key was reused can count, and a key shared by regular masternodes and evonodes can receive the wrong weight (`m3/r2-governance:rust/crates/dw-governance/src/tally.rs:6-19`). Thus `771 net yes`, the funding line/margin, budget allocation and even a specific node's “current vote” are not universally exact. The G7 mainnet comparison covers its measured sample, not these cases. Require a verified outpoint-to-type mapping for exact labels, or surface an explicit estimate/unknown state and avoid a definitive Passing/Over budget claim when ambiguity can cross the threshold.

4. **Major — Credit withdrawal has no ambiguous-result state.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:982-990` (`mn-withdraw`). The sheet specifies loading, unreachable, too little and completion, but a Platform transition can be accepted while its acknowledgement or result wait fails. The pinned Platform wallet returns `MasternodeWithdrawalUnconfirmed` and says it **must not be retried** until the claimable balance is reread, or a second withdrawal can be sent (`platform-wallet` at `ebe37f8a`, `src/wallet/masternode_withdrawal.rs:293-300`). Add a persistent “Withdrawal sent; checking its result” state, disable a second submission, refresh the Platform balance/nonce, and make the retry path available only after a definitive rejection.

5. **Major — Shared-session coin reservation omits the registration fee.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1284`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1299-1304` (`sh-join`), `docs/design/MG-00-masternodes-ux.md:138`. The participant is told exactly 300 Dash is reserved and the button accepts “Join with 300 Dash,” while D41 says that participant also funds a share of the network fee. Core requires the shared collateral output to be exactly 1,000 Dash (`src/evo/specialtxman.cpp:1203-1214`), so a session in which participants provide only their exact shares cannot also pay a positive transaction fee. Specify how each wallet reserves **share plus its fee contribution**, show that requirement before Join, and handle fee re-estimation before the locked terms/consent are signed.

6. **Major — Shared reward percentages overpromise when the operator has a cut.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1263-1268`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1297-1299` (`sh-join`). The general copy says a 300/1,000 share earns “30% of every payout,” while the same setup permits a nonzero operator share. Core subtracts the operator reward first and splits only the remaining owner reward by shares (`src/masternode/payments.cpp:162-179`); with a 5% operator cut, that share receives about 28.5% of the node's on-chain reward, subject to rounding. Describe it as “30% of the owner portion after the operator's cut” and show the effective expected split in the invitation and locked terms.

7. **Minor — The operator sheet contradicts self-run key custody.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1218-1227` (`mn-operator`). It says “Dash Wallet doesn't hold [the operator key]”; the spec also says a self-operator's BLS secret is derived in this wallet's keychain and revealed in Masternode keys (`docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:974-980,1149-1152,1226-1227`). Use custody-specific copy: a hosted operator keeps its secret on the node; a self-run wallet may derive it but this app does not use it to sign service updates or revocations, which are done on the node.

8. **Minor — The voting cutoff is presented as an exact acceptance deadline.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:885-888`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:996-1001`, `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1097-1105`. Core begins making a superblock trigger within the maturity window, rather than rejecting all votes at its first block (`src/governance/signing.cpp:38-47`). A later vote may still be accepted but may be too late to affect the next payout once a trigger exists. Call the displayed block an **estimated cutoff for the next payout**, and say what a vote cast after it can affect; do not imply the clock is the network's one definitive vote-acceptance deadline.

9. **Minor — The Confirming tooltip disagrees with the submission flow and Core.** `docs/design/UX-SPEC.md:1025-1026`. It says peers relay the proposal only after six fee confirmations, while D16 and Mine say the wallet submits after one. Core permits pre-acceptance/relay at one confirmation and requires six for full validity (`src/governance/object.h` constants `GOVERNANCE_MIN_RELAY_FEE_CONFIRMATIONS` and `GOVERNANCE_FEE_CONFIRMATIONS`; `src/rpc/governance.cpp:413-416`). Say “Submitted after the first confirmation; fully valid after six” and keep the two progress states distinct.
