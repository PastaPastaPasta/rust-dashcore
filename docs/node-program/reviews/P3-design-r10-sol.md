## Finding

### F1 — Major: section 7 can pair the old chain snapshot with the new `recent` cache

**Design:** P3 §2.5:612–625, §3.6:1368–1386 and 1428–1436, invariant I4:1483–1487, S1:2115–2125; D56. **Core:** `src/validation.cpp:4829–4837`; `src/net_processing.cpp:2228–2247, 6338–6383, 6409–6415, 6473–6496`.

**Schedule:** Let T be the active tip and the cached most-recent compact block. Peer P has requested HB compact blocks, knows T's parent A but not T, has `fSyncStarted = false`, and has U(T) pending in its announcement cell. Its `send_messages` head moves U(T), takes snapshot S at T, drains any `BlockChecked(T)`, then locks P's header cell and reads the old `announce_stamp` successfully. Pause it before the separate copy of `recent`. An RPC submits C extending T with a new header. V passes C5′, enters `NewPoWValidBlock(C)`, stores C's global stamp and changes `recent` to C, then blocks trying to take P's cell. P now copies `recent = C` while still using S at T. Section 6 sends initial `getheaders` with a locator from A; section 7 sends `cmpctblock(T)` by reading T from disk and drawing a *new* nonce because the cache says C. P's region sets `best_header_sent = T` and releases the cell. V's visit then sees that P knows T and sends `cmpctblock(C)`.

Core cannot produce that combination. `SendMessages` holds `cs_main` across sections 6–12. If it runs before C's accept, it sends the locator from A and uses the cached compact T (no new nonce), then C's callback may send C. If C's accept runs first, `m_best_header` is C, so section 6 starts from T; C's callback sees P did not yet know T and skips P, and section 7 may encode T from disk. The port combines the old locator with the new cache and then sends C. The stamp check cannot protect the later cache copy: V writes `recent` **before** it tries the cell. C5′ does not help because no `BlockChecked` need be outstanding when C begins.

**Required change:** make the section-7 cache copy and the stamp decision one validated observation. For example, after copying `recent` under P's cell, recheck the global stamp; on `Ahead`, release the cell and state, wait for publication, redrain and retry before any section runs. Add an S4-x/S1 interleaving paused between the first stamp read and the cache copy, and compare the locator, compact nonce/draw log, and C announcement with Core.

## C5′ ratification and requested checks

**Ratify C5′: yes**, subject to the independent F1 repair above. The overtaking it prevents is real: after another origin connects B, `BlockChecked(B)` may remain queued while V starts an RPC accept of D on B. Without C5′, V can enqueue `cmpctblock(D)` to P before the actor enqueues HB selection's `sendcmpct(1)` for B. Core executes `BlockChecked(B)` inline inside `ConnectTip(B)` (`validation.cpp:3427–3432`, `net_processing.cpp:2314–2343, 1397–1414`); D's tip-extending `NewPoWValidBlock` runs later under `cs_main` (`validation.cpp:4831–4832`). Core cannot put those frames in the reversed order. Design §2.4:486–494 and §3.6:1535–1539 correctly close it.

I found **no V → actor → V cycle** under the written contracts (§2.6:828–853, §3.8.1:1731–1789). If the actor waits for its own `ProcessBlock` reply, its wait drains the queue on the doorbell; C5′ defers that accept, and the drain releases the condition. A `ProcessHeaders` reply and `wait_published` are R/M-complete and V's C5 wait serves them. A full command lane is taken by the same wait. The actor drops a header cell before any V send or `wait_published`; section 7's `read_block` is specified to take no lock V holds in the call. A `lock_headers` retry releases its locks before the R round trip. At a C5 point, C7(b) has published every earlier signal, so the next drain can process the whole outstanding prefix. V is the sole signal producer; while C5′ parks, later outside-IBD accepts are deferred, so a busy peer cannot replenish the signal stream faster than the actor drains it. Progress still assumes ordinary scheduling and finite I/O, as Core does.

The IBD scope is sufficient for the **sendcmpct/cmpctblock order**: an earlier `BlockChecked` produced while IBD was true carries `ibd = true` and cannot select an HB peer (`net_processing.cpp:2334–2339`); once Core's finished-IBD latch is false, it stays false (`validation.cpp:1970–1990`). Even if loading ends between a pre-accept IBD test and `NewPoWValidBlock`'s guard, the earlier IBD-true entries have no HB `sendcmpct` to overtake. C5′ is broader than the minimum frame-order guard: it waits for every outstanding `BlockChecked`, including ones with no source or HB effect. Core has already run those callbacks inline and has no corresponding extra actor-drain latency. That timing residual belongs in §6.3's probe; it does not justify permitting the reversed frame order.

F1 from r9 closes structurally. For P2P `block`, RPC `submitblock`, and `generate*`, the same V `AcceptBlock` call invokes the whole callback before `SaveBlockToDisk` and ABC (§2.4:514–524, §2.5:605–641; Core `validation.cpp:4829–4837, 4867–4901`, `rpc/mining.cpp:159`). The callback uses V's tree, visits the connection table in Core's order, checks connection and HB/header guards per peer, and pushes to each send queue before it returns (`net_processing.cpp:2228–2267`, `net.h:1423–1440`). A held peer cell can delay one visit, but V still cannot write or connect B first. The V → cell → send-queue order and the stated no-V-wait-under-cell rule avoid a lock cycle; the global-stamp retry itself can finish once that accept publishes, although the cache copy after its check is F1 above.

F2 from r9 also closes. S4-x (7) now splits at the continuation's cell lock and the redrain snapshot (§6.1:2135–2150, §6.2:2210). Core releases `cs_main` after `ProcessNewBlockHeaders` and reacquires it for `UpdatePeerStateForReceivedHeaders` (`net_processing.cpp:3359–3362, 3270–3285, 3380`), allowing each stated before/after accept and connect case. The accepted-but-not-connected branch sets announcement time; the connected branch does not. The `Ahead` branch retries on the post-publication snapshot. No rejected DEC-236 relay-list, I0–I3, or M2 redrain outcome appeared in this delta apart from the new D56 cache race.

Its p50/p90/p99 and KS reports are useful timing evidence, including C5′'s delay, but **they alone do not sign off NFR-FP-02**. PRD:791 requires a held-out classifier AUC ≤ 0.55 over at least 200 sessions per implementation on regtest and testnet, with timing features; §6.3:2247–2255 appropriately leaves that decision to the classifier owner.
