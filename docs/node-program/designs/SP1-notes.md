# SP1: masternode sync, netfulfilled and mncache implementation notes and D-rows

SP1 (PLAN M5, size M) ports Core's `CMasternodeSync` and its `SyncManager` tick (the assets,
`ProcessTick`'s 6 s gate and timeouts, `SwitchToNextAsset`, the `IsBlockchainSynced`/`IsSynced`
gates and the chain-notification hooks), `CNetFulfilledRequestManager` with `netfulfilled.dat`,
and `CMasternodeMetaMan` with `mncache.dat`. Core line numbers are v24.0 rc.4's (pin
`375c64d2317`). This file records the implementer's decisions where the sources leave a choice
(IMPLEMENTER: "decide, record a D-row, continue").

## Where it lives

- `dashd-masternode::sync`: `MasternodeSync` (`masternode/sync.{h,cpp}`): the asset, attempt and
  bump state on atomics, `Reset`, `SwitchToNextAsset`, `AcceptedBlockHeader`, `NotifyHeaderTip`,
  `UpdatedBlockTip`, and the `NodeSyncNotifier` (`SyncReset`/`SyncFinished`).
- `dashd-masternode::sync_manager`: `SyncManager` (`node/sync_manager.{h,cpp}`): `ProcessTick`,
  `RequestGovernanceObjectVotes`, the `SyncNet` view of the connection and peer managers, the
  `GovernanceSync` seam (GV1b) and `EmptyGovernance`, its stand-in.
- `dashd-masternode::sync_net` (feature `net`): the `ssc` registry handler and `CoreSyncNet`, the
  tick's `SyncNet` over P2a's `NetCore`.
- `dashd-masternode::netfulfilled`, `::meta`: the two managers and their files.
- `dashd-masternode::flatdb`: Core's `CFlatDB` for rdashd's flat files, now shared with SP0's
  `sporks.dat`.
- `dashd-p2p::processing::logic::RequestedObjects`: `PeerGetRequestedObjectCount`.
- `dashd-node`: construction, the notification listener, cache load/store, the scheduler tasks
  and the overlay state (D9-D13).
- `contrib/node/sp1b-timing/` (SP1b, DEC-562): the timer KS harness `timing.py`, its nightly
  wrapper `nightly.sh` and the user units to install it (D18-D22).

| D19 | **Samples are independent: one session, one node process, one sample per measure.** Each session starts a fresh regtest node with its own datadir at real time, and gives at most one value of each measure. Values of different measures from one session are never pooled. Before `submitblock` and before `mnsync reset` the session waits U(0, 6) s from its own generator (seeded by the run seed and session id), so the reference moment's phase against the node's 1 s scheduler, its integer-second `GetTime()` and its 6 s gate is uniform. It does not depend on how long the node took to start, which differs between dashd and rdashd. The scheduler gives the next session to the (node, kind) pair with the fewest good and running sessions, so dashd and rdashd sessions interleave session by session (ABAB…, never in blocks) through the run and share the box's load. Session ids are given at dispatch from the dispatched count, so each (node, kind) holds one residue of the id mod 4. Every dashd session has its rdashd partner dispatched about 1 s away and in the same load window. The manager's check of the acceptance run (DEC-583) found KS D 0.0001 between the two nodes' dispatch positions, and no consistent drift from the first to the last quarter. Heavy load still adds noise, which costs power, hence D22's ceiling during the run. A failed session (timeouts, an unexpected log) is written to `errors.tsv` and replaced, up to a cap per (node, kind) of 20 + 2% of the target. `ks` fails any (node, kind) whose failures exceed 1% of its sessions, since a failure that tracks the timing would bias the samples left. Each node gets its own random RPC password, and a node that fails to start is killed, so a later session on its ports cannot reach it. | Successive samples from one process share its tick grid, so they would be correlated, and KS assumes i.i.d. samples. Without the random wait, the delay would measure start-up speed rather than the timer. |

| D21 | **The verdict compares the samples at 10 ms resolution, with a permutation p-value; the raw KS is reported beside it.** `ks` rounds each value to `--resolution` (0.01 s) and takes p from 1,000 relabellings of the pooled samples (`ks_permutation`). The asymptotic p is conservative on tied data: on two draws from one three-point distribution it rejected 0.5% of the time, against a nominal 5% (validator). No FINISHED→`mempool` interval is sampled (DEC-562 item 1, dropped by the manager 10-27): Core requests `mempool` when the tick leaves BLOCKCHAIN, before FINISHED, behind `-syncmempool` and only from outbound peers that are not block-relay-only (at rc.4, Core: node/sync_manager.cpp:217 for the switch, :220-233 for the `-syncmempool` block, :227 for the push), so `first6` and `first30` are that delay. D is `dash-p2p-transport/tests/dashd_interop.rs`'s `ks_two` statistic (DEC-562 asked for that code's reuse): the selftest checks `ks_d` against its brute-force maximum over every point of an integer domain. Its critical-value threshold (α = 1e-6, no p-value) is not reused, since NFR-FP-03 gates on p > 0.05. The permutation p is exact in distribution, and the selftest checks that it finds a 2% shift of mass between two tied points. `ks` also prints D and the asymptotic p at full resolution. At microsecond resolution `gap_tick` compares per-step processing time: six scheduler steps cost about 0.9 ms on dashd and 0.7 ms on rdashd (6.0009 s against 6.0007 s in the pilot), which NFR-FP-03 allows ("processing latency may be lower than dashd's"). At 10 ms a wrong period, an extra or missing step, or a drift of tens of ms still moves the binned CDF by the share of samples it affects. What the resolution hides, by design: a tick-period difference within one 10 ms bin, for example 6.0009 s against 6.0045 s, shows only through the share of samples it moves across a bin edge. The continuous measures (`first6`, `first30`, `reset_tick`) spread over 6 s or more, so binning changes their D by at most about 0.002. D is taken after each tie group (`ks_d`). `first6` and `first30` start when `submitblock` returns, and the bump happens inside the call. The reported `submit6` and `submit30` record the call's duration, so a node-specific delay after the bump would show there: at 10,000 samples a constant shift moves D by about shift / 6 s. | The NFR names the timers, not the code's speed. The resolution is a stated parameter, and the raw columns show what it hides. |
| D22 | **The nightly is `contrib/node/sp1b-timing/nightly.sh` under the user units beside it; installing them is the manager's call.** The wrapper runs the KS selftest, then `run` (10,000 good sessions per node and kind, 128 at a time) under `taskset -c $SP1B_CPUS` (default 0-15, the cores cap) and `nice -n 10`, then `ks`, which exits 1 unless every gated measure passes. With `SP1B_MAX_LOAD` (the unit sets 40) it starts only once the 1-minute load is below it. Since r1 (DEC-583) `run` also starts no session while the load is at or above it: one worker waits for the load to fall, the rest queue behind it, and each pause and resume is logged with the run's offset and the load. A pause ends as soon as no session is left to schedule, so a finished run never waits on the load. The ceiling is the system's load, the run's own nodes included. At 128 jobs these add little: the acceptance run ended with its sessions still running at a 1-minute load of 6.75. A pause has no limit of its own, and the unit's `TimeoutStartSec=10h` bounds it, so a run killed there is a failed night. Each `samples.tsv` row carries its session's dispatch time and the 1-minute load then (columns 6 and 7; rows from before r1 read `-`), so a run can be stratified by load afterwards. `uptime.txt` records `uptime` at the run's start and end; `run-summary.json` adds the minute-by-minute load's minimum, mean and maximum. The acceptance run is taken that way (DEC-562's load rule); a run at higher load is development data, and a failing pair is rerun at low load before it counts as a divergence. `run-summary.json` records the wall time and CPU-hours (the harness plus every reaped node). `sp1b-timing-nightly.{service,timer}` follow the CH3a nightly's form (Type=oneshot, MemoryMax, idle IO, a fixed UTC time).
